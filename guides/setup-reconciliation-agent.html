GETTING STARTED

Setting up your first Reconciliation Agent

Revzio Editorial · Guide · 8–12 min read

This guide walks a controller or finance ops lead through the first productive hour with Orion’s Reconciliation Agent. By the end you will have sources connected, match policy set, and a first continuous run producing exceptions you can review in product.

Before you start

Have read access (or a service user) for at least one bank or processor feed and your ERP or subledger. Decide which entities and accounts are in scope for week one — start narrow (cash and one billing processor) so exception volume stays manageable while you tune rules.

  • Confirm who can approve overrides and who owns unmatched items.
  • List known timing differences (payout delays, FX, batch settlement) so they become policy, not noise.
  • Skim the Reconciliation Agent deep dive if your team needs line-level context before go-live.

Step 1 — Connect sources

In Revzio, open Integrations and connect the bank/processor and ERP connectors for your pilot entity. Validate that recent transactions land with source IDs intact. If you use NetSuite, follow the field-mapping checks in the NetSuite integration guide.

Step 2 — Configure matching rules

Start with high-confidence exact matches on amount + date window + reference. Add fuzzy rules only where your identifiers routinely diverge (for example remittance batch vs invoice). Set tolerances for cents and FX. Anything outside policy should route as an exception with explainability — see exception explainability.

Step 3 — Run the first reconciliation

Trigger a historical window (often 30–60 days) so the agent builds a baseline. Review auto-matched items sample for correctness, then work the exception queue. Assign owners and due dates so breaks do not pile up into month-end.

Step 4 — Sign-off and audit trail

Approve matches and overrides in product. Confirm each decision stores who, when, and why. That trail feeds SOX and external audit without a separate evidence hunt — covered further in audit trail and SOC 2 readiness.

What “done” looks like after week one

Daily matching is running, exception owners are clear, and your team is resolving breaks while context is fresh. Next, expand accounts and tighten auto-match rates. Pair with continuous reconciliation and the 3-day close guide when you are ready to pull prep into every day of the month.

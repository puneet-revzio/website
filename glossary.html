Finance & accounting glossary

Plain-English definitions for close, reconciliation, and accounting automation — written for controllers and CFOs.

ABCDEFGHIJMOPRT
A

Accounting automation

Using software to prepare recurring accounting work — matching, journals, checklists, and evidence — so people review exceptions instead of keying every line. Done well, it shortens close without removing human judgment on material items.

Accounting treatment

The specific method used to record a transaction under GAAP or IFRS — when to recognize revenue, how to classify an expense, or how to schedule a prepaid. Clear treatment rules are what agents need before anything can auto-post.

Accounts payable (AP)

Money your company owes suppliers for goods or services already received. Strong AP operations combine invoice capture, 3-way match, approvals, and clean ERP posting so payables don’t become a close surprise.

AP Agent →

Accounts receivable (AR)

Money customers owe you for invoiced goods or services. Finance teams watch AR for cash timing, credit risk, and unapplied cash that can block a clean month-end.

AR Agent →

Accounts receivable aging

A report that buckets open invoices by how long they’ve been outstanding (for example 0–30, 31–60, 61–90 days). Aging highlights collection risk and often drives who gets chased first.

Accounts receivable automation

Automating cash application, dispute routing, and collections prioritization so receivables clear faster with fewer manual remittance matches. The goal is lower DSO and fewer “where did this payment go?” threads.

Accrual automation

Building period-end accruals and amortization schedules from source data instead of rebuilt spreadsheets. Typical examples: payroll accruals, commissions, and prepaid expense run-off — with a trail auditors can follow.

Accruals Agent →

Agent orchestration

Coordinating multiple specialized agents across a workflow — matching, journals, AP/AR, close tasks — with policy gates, routing, and approvals. In finance, orchestration is as much about controls as speed.

ASC 606

The US GAAP revenue standard for customer contracts. It forces performance obligations, allocation, and timing rules that get painful at scale when billing and ERP diverge — especially with upgrades, credits, and usage.

Audit trail

A durable record of who changed what, when, and why — including supporting evidence. Modern close stacks produce the trail as work happens, not as a binder project after the books are late.

Controls & audit trails →
B

Balance sheet reconciliation

Confirming that balance sheet accounts agree to subledgers, bank statements, and supporting schedules. Continuous recon reduces the classic late-cycle surprise that stretches close by days.

Reconciliation Agent →

Bank reconciliation

Matching bank activity to GL cash postings for completeness and accuracy. Line-level matching beats totals-only recon, because totals can tie while duplicates and misclasses still hide underneath.

Reconciliation →
C

Cash application

Matching incoming payments to open invoices using remittance details, bank references, and customer history. High automation clears unmatched cash faster and keeps AR ready for close.

AR Agent →

Close calendar

The schedule of tasks, owners, due dates, and dependencies required to close the books. If status only lives in Slack, you don’t really have a calendar — you have hope.

Close Management Agent →

Close management

Orchestrating month-end work end-to-end: checklists, blockers, approvals, and evidence. The point is visibility and accountability so finance isn’t rediscovering status every afternoon.

Close workflow →

Continuous close

Running reconciliation and journal prep throughout the period so month-end becomes confirmation. If your close still starts with “wait for final files,” you’re still in a batch model — even with AI somewhere in the stack.

Continuous close →

Controller

The finance leader accountable for accounting operations, controls, and getting the books closed cleanly. Controllers usually own recon quality, journal review, and audit readiness.

D

Data ingestion

Connecting ERP, billing, banking, payroll, CRM, and warehouses into a normalized model agents can trust. Bad mappings here create false exceptions faster than any matching engine can fix.

Data ingestion →

Deferred revenue

Cash collected before revenue is earned. Schedules get messy with upgrades, credits, and usage — which is why deferred tie-outs still steal days even when cash recon looks fine.

Revenue Validation Agent →

DSO (days sales outstanding)

How long, on average, it takes to collect after a sale. AR automation usually improves DSO by applying cash faster and prioritizing the right collections work.

E

ERP (enterprise resource planning)

The system of record for accounting and operations (for example NetSuite or SAP). Close automation typically pulls from — and posts back to — the ERP under governed approvals.

Integrations →

Exception management

Triaging breaks automation can’t resolve: ranking by impact, assigning owners, capturing outcomes. Useful exceptions include a summary and likely cause — not just an unmatched amount in a CSV.

Exception explainability →
F

Financial close

The recurring process of finalizing financial statements for a period — recon, journals, accruals, reviews, and sign-off. Speed without evidence is just a faster way to surprise auditors.

Close workflow →

Flux analysis

Explaining why balances changed period over period. In an automated close, variance narratives should link to source evidence and reviewer sign-off — not a slide deck rebuilt from scratch.

Close Management Agent →
G

General ledger (GL)

The master accounting ledger where transactions and adjustments land. Reconciliation and journal automation exist to keep the GL accurate, explainable, and audit-ready.

Governance controls

Policies, approvals, RBAC, and logging that keep automation safe. In finance-grade systems, controls are part of the workflow — not a spreadsheet afterthought.

Audit & controls →
H

Human-in-the-loop (HITL)

Automation prepares the work; humans approve material actions (like posting journals or overriding matches). That split keeps judgment with finance while still removing spreadsheet grind.

HITL in finance →
I

Intercompany reconciliation

Tying out transactions between entities in the same group. It usually needs multi-entity matching, consistent policies, and clear ownership when balances don’t agree.

Reconciliation Agent →

Invoice processing

Receiving, validating, approving, and posting supplier invoices. Modern stacks add extraction, duplicate detection, and 3-way match so AP isn’t pure data entry.

Procure-to-pay →
J

Journal entry automation

Generating balanced, validated journals from source systems with reviewer gates and lineage to evidence. The win is reviewing prepared entries — not rebuilding them from exports every month.

Journal Entry Agent →
M

Month-end close

The monthly cycle to finalize statements. Teams that move prep into the month convert close from a scramble into a short confirmation window.

3-day close guide →

Multi-entity consolidation

Combining subsidiaries into consolidated reporting — mappings, FX, and intercompany eliminations included. Policy consistency across entities is usually the hard part, not the math.

Order-to-cash (O2C)

The path from invoice to cash collected and posted — including cash application, collections, and dispute handling. Clean O2C keeps revenue and cash stories aligned at close.

Policy-gated automation

Automation that runs inside written thresholds and routes everything else to humans. Amount-only auto-match without gates isn’t efficiency — it’s scaled mistakes.

Prepaid amortization

Expensing prepaid assets over time on a schedule. Automating the schedule cuts spreadsheet work and keeps amortization explainable for audit.

Procure-to-pay (P2P)

From purchase order to invoice to payment — typically including 3-way match, approvals, and ERP posting. Leakage shows up when mismatches aren’t caught early.

R

RBAC (role-based access control)

Permissions assigned by role — preparer, reviewer, admin — so segregation of duties is enforced by the system, not by memory.

Record-to-report (R2R)

Turning operational transactions into closed books and reports: recon, journals, accruals, review, and sign-off. It’s the spine of the financial close.

Revenue recognition

Deciding when and how revenue hits the statements — often under ASC 606. Complex billing models create drift between billing systems and ERP unless validation runs continuously.
